01 · The Story

Hi, I'm Ryan

If you've struggled with unwanted behaviors, or you just want the kind of bond with your dog you see in the movies — you're not alone. I was once exactly where you are.

I've loved dogs my whole life. At nine I taught our family's rottweiler mix to heel after watching my mom get dragged down the street. Later, my border collie Zeppelin and pittie mix Whiskey walked at my side for 832 miles across Arizona, 1,800+ miles of the Pacific Crest Trail, and countless other adventures.

Then in 2018 I brought home Skookums, a malinois, at eight weeks old — and quickly learned I didn't know as much as I thought. He was reactive to people, bikes, skateboards, anything fast or new. After months of trying everything online with no results, I reached out to a professional trainer. It was hands down the best thing I ever did.

That led me to a protection-sport club, mentorship under a training director with 40 years of experience, and a two-year live-in apprenticeship. Since then I've worked across the country with military and police K9, pet obedience, and behavior modification. Skookums went on to compete in French Ring — calm enough to be examined by a stranger.

I've been there

I know what it's like to feel frustrated and doubt your relationship with your dog. I also know how good it feels on the other side.

Our Philosophy

We build a language your dog and the person holding the leash both understand — when it's time to be a dog, and when it's time to settle.

From fearful, reactive, and aggressive dogs to happy-go-lucky puppies who just need guidance, the focus is always the same: a better bond and relationship with your best friend.

03 · The Work

Earned in protection sport

The methods behind every program were pressure-tested in French Ring — obedience, agility, and controlled protection work. It's where drive, clarity, and trust either hold up or they don't. This is the deep end, and it's where the fundamentals come from.

The hardest work always ends the same way — back in a bond.

Intensity is never the point. Whether it's a competition dog or the family pet, everything comes back to a dog who trusts the person holding the leash.
